---
title: Read a maintenance comparison
description: Work through the difference between repair spending and repair cost per kilometre.
---

In this exercise, you will compare repair spending across two years, account for
recorded distance, and write a conclusion supported by both measures.

Use the two graphs and their source tables in
[Why repair spending needs a distance comparison](/docs/maintenance-findings/).

## Establish the scope

Read the source caption beneath the first graph. Both years cover the same eight
selected buses, and each year runs from October to September.

Write down that scope before comparing the values. Your conclusion will concern
those buses over those years, rather than the whole operating fleet.

## Compare the repair charges

In the first graph's source table, compare the repair charges for the two years.
The later year has higher charges. Routine servicing and additional preventive
work are separate categories; leave them out of a comparison of repairs alone.

At this point, you can conclude that recorded repair spending increased. You
cannot yet tell whether the increase simply reflects more distance travelled.

## Account for distance

Read the recorded kilometres, then compare the rates in the second graph.
Each rate divides repair charges by kilometres for the same year and multiplies
the result by 1,000.

Both distance and repair charges increased, but repair charges grew faster.
The higher rate means the selected buses incurred more repair cost per recorded
kilometre in the later year.

## Write the conclusion

A conclusion supported by these records is:

> For the eight selected buses, repair spending increased between the two complete
> October–September years. Repair cost per 1,000 km also increased, so the rise in
> recorded distance alone does not account for the rise in spending.

The comparison does not identify the cause. Vehicle condition and individual
repair records would be needed to investigate why costs changed.

You have now distinguished a total, a rate, and a causal explanation. For another
comparison, follow [Compare report periods](/docs/compare-reports/).
