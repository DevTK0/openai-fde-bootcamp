---
title: Why a remaining queue does not always mean a full bus
description: Separate boarding events, stop-call queues, and capacity before drawing a conclusion.
---

Across **{fmt(operations.metrics.calls)} recorded stop calls**,
**{fmt(operations.metrics.queuedCalls)}** have a remaining queue. Only
**{fmt(operations.metrics.fullCalls)}** depart at their recorded capacity.
A queue left behind is therefore not, by itself, evidence that a bus was full.

## Keep the queue accounting categories separate

Within the supplied route-position windows, the records balance as follows:
initial queue plus new arrivals equals boardings plus the final remaining queue.

The **{fmt(operations.metrics.boardings)} boardings** are events, not unique people.
The **{fmt(operations.metrics.remainingQueue)} remaining** is summed across separate
route-position and date windows. It is not a count of people waiting simultaneously
at the end of one network-wide period.

## A stop call is only one observation

People arriving during a stop's dwell can miss the counting cutoff even when the
bus has space. Repeated calls can also observe the same waiting group. Adding all
remaining queues across calls would not produce a count of unique passengers.
The model does not include abandonment or switching to another route.

## Passenger accounts have a different scope

Passenger accounts can identify a journey worth checking. They do not, on their
own, establish the number of people affected across the network or a change in
customer retention.
