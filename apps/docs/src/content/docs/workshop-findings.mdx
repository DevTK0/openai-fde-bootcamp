Two maintenance requests are proposed for 09:00 on 19 October 2026. Each needs a
bay. Together they need three technicians, while the workshop has one bay and two
technicians available at a time.

## The clash exists before considering service cover

The requests exceed both bay and technician capacity at their common start time.
The resource comparison identifies a scheduling conflict; it does not approve a
new schedule or make a replacement vehicle available.

One request has a proposed end time of 11:00 and the other 13:00. Moving a request
may remove the overlap, but a workable plan also needs enough time, staff, duty
cover, and a confirmed release for the vehicle.

## A tentative end time is not a release

Only one additional cover vehicle is offered in the supplied planning records.
The protected service duties and named crews still need to be covered. A vehicle
cannot be treated as ready merely because its tentative workshop end time has passed.

The proposed times remain planning inputs until Engineering confirms release.

## Keep plans separate from outcomes

The graph shows requested resources, not resources consumed by completed work.
It does not establish which plan was chosen, whether either job finished, or what
service disruption occurred. The same distinction applies to festival proposals
and incident assumptions in the supplied planning records.
