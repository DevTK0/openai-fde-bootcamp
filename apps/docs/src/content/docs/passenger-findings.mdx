The supplied records contain six selected passenger accounts received by phone
or through the web. They describe particular experiences. They are not a complete
survey of passengers or a representative measure of service quality.

## The channel counts describe this selection

More of these selected accounts came through the web than by phone. That does not
establish which channel all passengers prefer or which produces more complaints
relative to the number of people using it.

## A journey match identifies an event, not its cause

Stop details, journey windows, and supplied identifiers help identify a possible
match between a passenger account and an operating journey. Expected
journeys use scheduled departure times; observed journeys use actual departures.

A matched trip helps locate the event. It does not establish why an experience
occurred or confirm every detail of the account. A comfort report, for example,
needs condition and maintenance evidence before it can support a diagnosis.

## Boardings are not customer retention

Repeated boarding observations can show activity on recorded dates. They do not
identify which people returned, why someone stopped travelling, or whether a
service change caused growth.

These accounts provide evidence of reported experiences. Without a representative
sample or a passenger denominator, they cannot establish how common those
experiences are.
